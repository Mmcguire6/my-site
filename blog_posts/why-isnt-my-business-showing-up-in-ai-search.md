A year ago, almost nobody asked a chatbot to find them a plumber. Now, roughly **45% of people say they use AI — ChatGPT, Google's AI Mode — to find or choose a local business,** up from just **6% a year earlier.** That's not a niche habit anymore. It's close to half the homeowners in your town.

If you've spent the last while making sure you show up in the Google Maps 3-pack — good, keep doing that — but a second channel has opened up right alongside it, and almost nobody has touched it yet. Here are five reasons your business is probably invisible on it, and how to fix each one.

## 1. Your customers already started asking AI, not just Google

**The pain:** In the same survey, **ChatGPT alone was used by roughly 31% of consumers** for business recommendations over the past year, and **Google's own AI Mode by about 23%** — while Google's overall share of local-business discovery slipped from **83% down to 71%** over that same stretch. None of that means Google search is going away. It means a real, fast-growing slice of the people who'd normally search "roofer near me" are now typing that same question into a chatbot instead — and most business owners have never once checked whether they show up in the answer.

**The fix:** Actually test it. Open ChatGPT or Google's AI Mode and ask the exact question a customer would — "who's a good roofer in Edmonton" or "furnace repair near me in Red Deer" — and see whether you're mentioned at all, and who gets named instead. Fifteen minutes of doing that tells you more than any ranking report.

## 2. A regular Google search might not even show you a spot to rank for anymore

**The pain:** Even businesses doing everything right on Google Maps are losing ground here. Recent local-search data puts **AI Overviews appearing on roughly 68% of local-intent searches**, compared with a plain local map pack showing on about 39% of the same searches. So even if you rank first in the 3-pack, the customer may never scroll down to it — Google's AI has already summarized an answer above it, and if you're not part of that summary, you're invisible before your ranking even matters.

**The fix:** You can't SEO your way into an AI Overview the same way you climb the map pack. It comes down to giving Google's AI a page it can confidently quote a direct answer from — a sentence that plainly states your service, your area, and what it costs — instead of vague marketing copy that never actually answers the question being asked.

## 3. Your details don't match closely enough for AI to trust you

**The pain:** AI tools build their answer the same cautious way Google does: by cross-checking your website against your Google Business Profile and whatever review sites and directories mention you, looking for agreement. If your hours, address, or services are written a little differently in three different places, an AI system would rather leave you out of the answer than risk quoting something wrong. It's the same problem [that keeps businesses off Google Maps](why-isnt-my-business-on-google-maps.html) — just now there's a second system running the same check.

**The fix:** Pick one exact version of your business name, address, phone number, and hours, and make sure it's identical on your website, your Google Business Profile, and every directory that lists you. It's the same fix either way — you're just now getting credit for it with two audiences instead of one.

## 4. Your website doesn't actually say anything an AI can quote

**The pain:** A person skimming your homepage will forgive vague copy and hunt for your phone number anyway. An AI model won't — it's looking for a specific sentence it can lift and cite: your service, your city, roughly what it costs. A homepage that says "Quality Service Since 1995," or a single catch-all Services page listing everything you do in one paragraph, gives it nothing usable. It just moves on to the next business's page that actually says "we install furnaces in Red Deer, starting around a specific price."

**The fix:** Build a real page for each service you offer and each town you serve, written in plain, specific language — [the same service-and-city pages that win contractor sites their leads](contractor-website-not-getting-leads.html) are exactly what an AI model needs in order to quote you with confidence instead of skipping you for a competitor who spelled it out.

## 5. There's nothing on your site telling AI what you actually are

**The pain:** Underneath the words on a page, there's a layer of code called structured data (or "schema markup") that spells out — explicitly, for a machine — what kind of business you are, where you're located, what you charge, and what customers think of you. Without it, an AI system is left guessing at all of that from ordinary sentences, the same way it would from a page with no labels on anything. Most small-business sites don't have it at all, which means most of your competitors are handing AI tools a guess instead of an answer.

**The fix:** Add LocalBusiness schema — a standard, well-documented bit of structured data — to your homepage and service pages. It's a small, mostly invisible fix, but it's part of how we build [local SEO](../features/local-seo.html) into every site from day one, so the same groundwork that helps you rank on Maps also hands AI tools a clean, trustworthy answer instead of a shrug.

## Where to start

In order of impact:

1. **Go test it yourself** — ask ChatGPT or Google's AI Mode the exact question a customer would, and see if you show up.
2. **Get your details consistent everywhere** — website, Google Business Profile, directories, all matching exactly.
3. **Write real, specific service and city pages** — plain language an AI can actually quote, not vague marketing copy.
4. **Add structured data** — a small technical fix that tells AI plainly what you are.

None of this replaces ranking on Google Maps — it sits alongside it. But it's a channel almost nobody in your trade has touched yet, which means the businesses that fix it first are the ones AI recommends by default for a long while after.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why doesn't my business show up when I ask ChatGPT for a recommendation?

Usually it's one of a few things: your business details aren't consistent across your website, Google Business Profile, and directories; your website doesn't state your services and area in plain, specific language an AI can quote; or there's no structured data telling AI plainly what your business is. AI tools would rather leave an uncertain business out of the answer entirely than risk recommending the wrong one.

### Is it worth optimizing for AI search if I already rank well on Google Maps?

Yes — they're different, growing audiences. Roughly 45% of people now say they use AI to find a local business, up from just 6% a year earlier, and AI Overviews now appear on a majority of local searches. Ranking well on Maps doesn't automatically mean you show up in an AI answer; the underlying signals overlap, but it's worth checking both separately.

### What is structured data or schema markup, in plain terms?

It's a small, invisible piece of code added to your website's pages that spells out facts about your business — name, address, services, hours, reviews — in a format machines can read directly, instead of having to guess from ordinary sentences. It doesn't change how your site looks to a visitor; it just gives AI tools and search engines a clean, confirmed answer instead of an interpretation.

### How do I check if my business shows up in AI search results?

Open ChatGPT or Google's AI Mode and ask the question a real customer would ask — "best plumber in Calgary" or "who does furnace repairs near me" — using your own city. If you're not mentioned, note who is, and take a look at what their website and Google Business Profile do differently. That quick check is the simplest audit there is.

### Does this replace normal local SEO and Google Maps ranking?

No — it builds on it. Consistent business details, real service and city pages, and a fast, trustworthy website help you rank on Google Maps and get recommended by AI for largely the same reasons. Think of AI search as a new channel riding on top of the local SEO work you should already be doing, not a separate project.
