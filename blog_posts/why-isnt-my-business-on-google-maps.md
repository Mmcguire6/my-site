When someone in your town searches "roofer near me" or "furnace repair Edmonton," Google shows a map with three businesses pinned under it. That box — the local "map pack" — takes the **majority of the clicks** for local searches. The businesses in it get the calls. Everyone below the fold splits the scraps.

So if you're not showing up on Google Maps, you're not just ranking low — you're nearly invisible to the customers standing closest to you, ready to hire today. Here are the seven reasons it happens, and how to fix each one.

## 1. Your profile isn't verified — or it got suspended

**The pain:** Google won't rank a business it can't confirm is real. If your Google Business Profile was never verified, or it got suspended for a rule you didn't know about (a virtual address, keyword-stuffed name, or a category mismatch), it simply won't appear in the map results at all.

**The fix:** Search your exact business name. If no profile shows, [claim and verify one](https://www.google.com/business/) — it's free. If it's suspended, fix the cause (real address, real name, correct category) and request reinstatement. Nothing else on this list matters until your profile is live and verified.

## 2. Your primary category is wrong or too vague

**The pain:** Your category is the single biggest factor Google uses to decide *which* searches you show up for. Pick "Contractor" when you're a "Roofing Contractor," and you'll lose to every competitor who chose the specific one.

**The fix:** Set your **primary category** to the most specific match for your core service — "Roofing Contractor," "HVAC Contractor," "Plumber" — and add secondary categories for the other services you offer. Specific beats broad every time.

## 3. Your name, address, and phone don't match across the web

**The pain:** Google cross-checks your name, address, and phone number (your "NAP") against directories, your website, and social profiles. If your address is written three different ways or an old phone number is still floating around, Google gets confused about which version to trust — and a confused Google ranks you lower.

**The fix:** Pick one exact format for your business name, address, and phone, and make it identical everywhere — your website, Google profile, Facebook, and every directory. Consistency is a ranking signal most competitors ignore.

## 4. Your profile is bare

**The pain:** A profile with just a name and phone number looks abandoned next to a competitor with photos, full hours, a service list, and a written description. Google reads that completeness as "this business is active and trustworthy" — and ranks it accordingly.

**The fix:** Fill in everything: services, hours, service areas, a real description, and — crucially — recent photos of your actual work. Businesses that post photos regularly consistently outperform those that don't.

## 5. You have too few reviews, or they're all old

**The pain:** Reviews are one of the strongest local ranking signals *and* the thing homeowners judge you on. A profile with 6 reviews from two years ago loses to the one with a steady trickle of recent ones — twice over, in rankings and in trust.

**The fix:** Build a simple habit of asking every happy customer for a review, and make it one tap: a short link you text them when the job's done. Steady and recent beats a big pile of old.

## 6. Your website gives Google nothing local to work with

**The pain:** Google ranks your map listing partly on what your *website* says. If your site is one generic "Services" page with no mention of the specific towns you serve, Google has little reason to connect you to "furnace repair Red Deer." Your profile and your site are supposed to reinforce each other; a thin site leaves your listing doing all the work alone.

**The fix:** Build real pages for each service and the areas you cover, so your site backs up your map listing with local relevance — the same [service-and-city pages that win you leads](contractor-website-not-getting-leads.html). Make sure that site is fast, too: Google factors page experience into local results, so a [slow site](why-is-my-website-so-slow.html) drags your ranking down. This is the heart of [local SEO](../features/local-seo.html).

## 7. You're new, distant, or duplicated

**The pain:** Three things you can't fully control still matter. **Distance:** Google favours businesses near the searcher, so you'll rank strongest in your immediate area. **Age:** a brand-new profile takes time to earn trust. **Duplicates:** old or duplicate listings for the same business split your signals and confuse rankings.

**The fix:** Find and remove or merge any duplicate listings. Then be patient but consistent — a new profile usually starts ranking for discovery searches within **2 to 8 weeks,** faster if it's complete and gathering reviews. Distance you handle by building those city-specific pages so you're relevant across your whole service area, not just your home postal code.

## Where to start

In order of impact:

1. **Verify (or un-suspend) your profile** — nothing works until this is done.
2. **Fix your primary category and complete every field** — the fastest ranking wins.
3. **Get consistent NAP everywhere** — quiet but powerful.
4. **Build a steady review habit** — compounds forever.
5. **Back it with a fast, local website** — real service and city pages that reinforce your listing.

Ranking on Google Maps isn't luck or magic — it's a checklist most local businesses never finish. Finish it, and you become one of the three the whole town sees first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my business not showing up on Google Maps at all?

The most common reason is that your Google Business Profile isn't verified, or it's been suspended. Google won't show a business it can't confirm is real. After that, the usual culprits are a missing or wrong category, an incomplete profile, or inconsistent name/address/phone details across the web. Start by searching your exact business name — if nothing appears, verification is your first job.

### How long does it take to show up on Google Maps?

A newly verified profile usually starts appearing for your business name within a few days, but ranking for the searches that actually bring leads — "your service + your city" — typically takes **2 to 8 weeks.** The timeline depends on how competitive your area is, how complete your profile is, and how quickly you gather recent reviews and activity.

### How do I rank higher in the Google Maps 3-pack?

Google ranks local results on relevance, distance, and prominence. In practice that means: choose a specific primary category, complete every field with photos and services, keep your name/address/phone consistent everywhere, earn steady recent reviews, and back it all with a fast website that has real pages for each service and town you serve. Do those consistently and you climb toward the top three.

### Do reviews really affect Google Maps rankings?

Yes — reviews are one of the strongest local signals, and recency matters as much as volume. A steady trickle of new reviews outperforms a large pile of old ones. They also drive the click once you rank, since homeowners compare star ratings before they call. Asking every happy customer with a one-tap link is the simplest high-impact habit you can build.

### Does my website affect my Google Maps ranking?

It does. Google uses your website to judge how relevant and trustworthy your listing is, so a fast site with dedicated pages for each service and location strengthens your map ranking, while a slow, thin one holds it back. Your profile and your website are meant to reinforce each other — improving both together beats working on either alone.
