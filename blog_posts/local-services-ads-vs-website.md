Google Local Services Ads run roughly **$53 to $150 per lead** depending on your trade and city — and industry data puts the average booking rate at just **43.9%.** That means over half of every lead you pay for never turns into a job, and the moment you stop paying, the leads stop too.

A lot of Alberta trades treat Local Services Ads (the "pay per lead" badge at the top of Google) as the whole marketing plan, and skip the website entirely — it feels like the faster, simpler option. It can work. But it's renting attention, not owning it, and the rent keeps going up. Here are six reasons a website still has to be part of the plan, even if you're running ads too.

## 1. You're renting the leads, not owning them

**The pain:** Every Local Services Ads lead costs you money the moment it lands, and that's it — no residual value, no compounding. Stop paying for a month because cash is tight, and your leads stop that same day. You're back to zero, with nothing built to show for however many thousand dollars you've spent.

**The fix:** A website is the opposite kind of asset. The furnace-repair page you write once for Red Deer keeps showing up in search results for years, at no extra cost per lead. You can still run ads on top of it — but the website is what's still working for you the month you can't afford to run them.

## 2. The cost per lead is higher than most owners expect — and it keeps climbing

**The pain:** A February 2026 benchmark covering 888 contractors and roughly $6.72 million in Local Services Ads spend put the blended cost per lead at around **$53** — ranging from about **$39 for electrical** up to **$57 for plumbing** and **$59 for drain and sewer work.** Run standard Google Search ads instead and the same data puts you closer to **$104 per lead**, climbing to roughly **$149** on non-branded searches. Either way, every single lead has a price tag attached, forever.

**The fix:** A website's cost doesn't scale with how many leads you get. Once it's built and ranking, an extra inquiry from organic search or Google Maps costs you nothing — which is exactly why pairing ads with a [fast, lead-focused website](contractor-website-not-getting-leads.html) lowers your real cost per lead over time instead of leaving it flat for as long as you advertise.

## 3. The platform can change the rules on you overnight

**The pain:** You don't control Local Services Ads — Google does, and it's been moving fast. In October 2025, Google retired the "Google Guaranteed" money-back badge and folded it into a single "Google Verified" badge, dropping the refund guarantee that used to come with it. Now Google is migrating the entire Local Services Ads dashboard into its Performance Max system starting August 2026, which retires the standalone dashboard, drops your historical performance reports, and removes manual control over how much you pay per lead.

**The fix:** Nobody can migrate, retire, or reprice your own website out from under you. It's the one piece of your marketing that isn't subject to a platform's roadmap — which matters most for the parts of your business, like [what you're paying for marketing](small-business-website-cost-alberta.html), that you actually need to be able to plan around.

## 4. A paid lead still has to be caught, or you've wasted the money

**The pain:** That 43.9% average booking rate cuts both ways — it also means a majority of leads you paid for don't close, and a slow response is one of the biggest reasons why. Someone clicks the ad, you already paid for that click, and if the call goes to voicemail or the message sits for a day, you've paid full price for a lead that was never going to book.

**The fix:** Whatever brings the lead in — an ad, a Google listing, or your own site — the thing that actually converts it is a fast, reliable follow-up. That's the same [lead follow-up](../features/lead-follow-up.html) system that turns a missed call into a text instead of a dead end; it's just as valuable protecting a lead you paid $50 for as one that came in free.

## 5. It doesn't work for every kind of local business

**The pain:** Local Services Ads is built around home-service trades — plumbers, electricians, HVAC, roofers, and similar categories. If you run a local shop, a clinic, a salon, or anything outside that list, there's often no "pay per lead" badge available to you at all, no matter your budget.

**The fix:** A website has no eligibility list. Every Alberta small business, trade or not, can build one, show up in local search, and take bookings — which is why it has to be the foundation under any paid-ads strategy, not an optional extra bolted on after.

## 6. It builds nothing that compounds

**The pain:** Run Local Services Ads for two years and you'll have generated a lot of leads — and exactly zero long-term search presence, because the ad budget doesn't build your rankings on Google Maps or in regular search results. Stop the campaign and there's nothing left behind it.

**The fix:** A website paired with ongoing [local SEO](../features/local-seo.html) gets a little stronger every month — more pages, more reviews, more history for Google to trust. Two years in, that compounding is the difference between still needing to pay for every single lead and having a steady stream that shows up for free.

## Where to start

If you're already running Local Services Ads, keep running them — just don't let them be the whole plan. In order of payoff:

1. **Build the website first, even a simple one** — it's the asset that still works the month you can't afford ads.
2. **Point your ad budget at closing what you already pay for** — a fast follow-up system protects the leads you're spending real money on.
3. **Let your website do the compounding work** — service and city pages that keep ranking long after any campaign ends.
4. **Treat ads as a supplement, not a replacement** — the businesses that do best usually run both, with the website carrying the weight ads can't.

Paid leads can be a useful way to fill the calendar short-term. They were never meant to be the only thing standing between your business and the next job.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Google Local Services Ads worth it for a small Alberta business?

They can be, especially to fill gaps quickly — but they're an ongoing expense with no residual value once you stop paying, and industry data puts the average booking rate at under half of paid leads. Most trades do better treating ads as a supplement to a website rather than a replacement for one, since the website keeps producing leads even during months you're not running a campaign.

### How much do Local Services Ads cost per lead?

Recent industry benchmarks put the blended average around $53 per lead, ranging from roughly $39 for electrical work up to around $59 for drain and sewer jobs — noticeably cheaper than standard Google Search ads, which run closer to $104 to $149 per lead. Your actual cost depends heavily on your trade, city, and competition.

### Do I still need a website if I have Local Services Ads?

Yes. Local Services Ads only covers certain home-service trades, pulls its information from your Google Business Profile rather than anything you control directly, and stops producing leads the moment you stop paying. A website works for any type of business, keeps ranking in regular search and Google Maps results long-term, and is the one piece of your marketing a platform change can't take away from you.

### What happened to the Google Guaranteed badge?

In October 2025, Google merged its Google Guaranteed, Google Screened, and License Verified badges into a single "Google Verified" badge and dropped the money-back guarantee that used to come with it. Google is also migrating the entire Local Services Ads dashboard into its Performance Max system starting in August 2026, which is a reminder that the platform can change the terms of the deal without much notice.
