Roughly **4 to 7% of Google Business Profile views turn into a website click.** That number gets used to argue the opposite of what it should — "see, nobody wants your website, the free listing is enough." Read it the other way and it says something different: out of everyone who looked you up on Google, this is your most motivated group. They liked what they saw enough to want more. If there's nothing to click through to, you lose the exact customers who were closest to calling.

A lot of Alberta trades and local shops run on a free Google Business Profile alone and figure that covers it. It covers being *found*. It doesn't cover being *chosen*. Here are six reasons a listing by itself keeps quietly losing you jobs — and what a real website fixes.

## 1. Most people still won't commit on a listing alone

**The pain:** A Google Business Profile shows a name, a star rating, a few photos, and a phone number. That's enough to get you on a shortlist — it's rarely enough to get you the job. Roughly **1 in 4 shoppers researching a new local business are unlikely to visit or call it at all if it has no online presence beyond a directory listing,** and separately, consumers rate a business website as more credible than a social or directory profile alone. For a job that costs real money — a roof, a furnace, a bathroom — "trust me, I'm a five-star listing" isn't the same as showing your work.

**The fix:** Give the homeowner somewhere to go past the stars: real project photos, the services you actually run, and a straight answer on how the job works. That's what turns "looks fine" into "I'll call this one."

## 2. The 4-7% who click through are your best leads — and you're sending them nowhere

**The pain:** Most people who see your profile never click "website" — they're just checking hours or a phone number. But the small slice who do click are the ones who wanted more than a listing could give them: proof, pricing, past work, a reason to pick you over the next name on the map. Without a website, that click either goes to a "coming soon" page, a Facebook feed, or nothing — and they land on a competitor's site instead, since Google shows several businesses at once.

**The fix:** Make sure that click lands somewhere worth landing. A few solid pages — home, services, a handful of real jobs, a way to get in touch — is enough to hold onto the customers who were already halfway to hiring you.

## 3. A profile can't show the work that actually sells the job

**The pain:** Google Business Profile gives you a photo grid, not a story. There's no room to walk someone through a roof replacement in Edmonton start to finish, show before-and-afters from a specific job, or explain what's included in a furnace tune-up versus a full install. Everything gets flattened into the same small thumbnails as every competitor's listing.

**The fix:** Build out a page per service with real photos and a plain explanation of what the customer gets. That's the kind of detail that closes a job a bare listing never could.

## 4. You don't own it — Google does

**The pain:** A Google Business Profile can be suspended, edited by someone else, or caught in a verification hold, and there's very little you can do but wait it out. Rapid edits, a shared address, or a category change can all trip Google's spam checks even when you've done nothing wrong. When that happens, your *only* online presence disappears overnight — no backup, no way to reach the customers who were about to call.

**The fix:** Treat your website as the one piece of your online presence you actually control. Your profile can go down for review; your website keeps running, keeps ranking, and keeps taking calls the whole time.

## 5. An incomplete profile performs worse — and the website field is part of "complete"

**The pain:** Google rewards fully filled-out profiles: they get roughly **7 times more clicks and 70% more in-store visits** than sparse ones. The website field is one of the fields Google checks. Leave it blank, and you're not just missing a website — you're handing Google one more reason to treat your profile as less complete, less active, and less worth showing first.

**The fix:** Fill in every field a Google Business Profile offers, website link included. It's a small box to tick, and it's one more small signal working in your favour instead of against you.

## 6. Every competitor's listing looks the same — a website is the one place you don't

**The pain:** Open three roofers' Google profiles in the same town and they're nearly identical: same layout, same star widget, same handful of categories. There's nowhere on a listing to explain why you're different — your guarantee, your financing, your actual process — so price and star count end up doing all the deciding.

**The fix:** A website is the one channel where you set the terms: your story, your guarantee, clear pricing expectations, and one obvious call to action instead of a generic "Website" button buried under five competitors' listings.

## Where to start

You don't need to rebuild everything on day one. In order of payoff:

1. **Get a real website live** — even a few clean pages beats a dead end for the customers already motivated enough to click through.
2. **Fill in the website field on your profile** — and every other field while you're there.
3. **Add proof** — real job photos, a plain explanation of your services, and how to reach you.
4. **Build out service pages over time** — this is also the foundation of [local SEO](../features/local-seo.html) and helps you [show up higher on Google Maps](why-isnt-my-business-on-google-maps.html), not just look better once someone clicks through.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I really need a website if I already have a Google Business Profile?

Yes, if you want to convert the people who are most interested. Your profile gets you found in local search, but it can only show a name, a rating, and a few photos. The customers who click through to "website" are your most motivated leads, and without a site, that click leads nowhere — usually straight to a competitor.

### Isn't a Google Business Profile free, so why pay for a website too?

Free and sufficient aren't the same thing. A profile is a listing you don't own or control — Google can suspend or hold it for review with little warning — and it can't show your work, your process, or why you're different from the next business in the map results. A website is the one piece of your online presence that's actually yours.

### Will a website help my Google Business Profile perform better?

Yes. Completed profiles — website link included — get roughly 7 times more clicks and 70% more in-store visits than incomplete ones, according to Google's own profile-completeness data. Filling in the website field is one of the simplest ways to make your profile look more active and trustworthy to Google.

### What should I put on my website if I already have a Google Business Profile with reviews?

Focus on what your profile can't show: real photos from specific jobs, a page per core service, a plain description of how the work gets done, and one clear way to get in touch. Think of the profile as the shortlist and the website as the reason you get picked off it.
