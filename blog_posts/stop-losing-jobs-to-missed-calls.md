The phone rings while you're on a roof, under a sink, or driving to your next job. You can't answer. By the time you call back an hour later, they've already booked the contractor who picked up.

That one missed call just cost you the job. And it's not a rare event. Invoca's 2024 research on calls to home-service businesses found that **about 1 in 4 calls (27%) go unanswered** ([Invoca](https://invoca.com/blog/how-much-missed-sales-calls-cost-home-services-businesses)). Some of those callers leave a voicemail. Plenty don't. They just dial the next name on Google.

Add that up over a month and it's real money: work that simply rang through to voicemail. Here's why it happens, and five ways to stop it.

## Why the phone rings when you can't answer it

You're not missing calls because you're lazy. You're missing them because you can't be on a ladder and on the phone at the same time. The three biggest gaps:

- **You're on the job.** Hands full, loud site, gloves on. The one time of day a customer calls is the one time you can't talk.
- **It's after hours.** Plenty of homeowners call in the evening or on the weekend, when they finally sit down and deal with that leak. A voicemail box is the only thing working that shift.
- **Everyone calls at once.** A storm rolls through, ten roofs need looking at, and the calls stack up faster than one person can answer.

None of that is a character flaw. It's just the reality of running a trade. The problem is what happens to the call you miss.

## Why voicemail doesn't save you

Voicemail feels like a safety net. It isn't. A homeowner with a leak is often calling more than one contractor, and every hour you wait, the lead gets colder.

The research on this is blunt. When Harvard Business Review audited how 2,241 US companies handled online leads, the firms that got back within an hour were **nearly 7 times as likely to have a real conversation with the decision maker** as firms that waited even one hour longer, and more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited a day or more ([HBR, 2011](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)).

So a missed call isn't a message waiting for you. It may be a customer already talking to your competitor. Which means the fix isn't "check voicemail more often." It's making sure the call gets a response *now,* even when you can't give it. Here are five ways to do exactly that, roughly in order of payoff.

## 1. Call back within 5 minutes, every time

Speed-to-lead is one of the biggest levers you have. The Lead Response Management Study by InsideSales.com and MIT found that "the odds of qualifying a lead if called in 5 minutes versus 30 minutes drop 21 times" ([Lead Response Management Study, 2007](https://www.mortech.com/hs-fs/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf)). Call back fast and you reach the customer while they're still holding the phone and still deciding.

**The catch:** you can't reliably hit a five-minute callback when you're working. Which is exactly why the next four matter.

## 2. Text back every missed call, automatically

This is the highest-payoff fix on the list. The moment a call goes unanswered, an automatic text fires back to the caller: *"Sorry we missed you, this is [Your Company]. Can we help with a quote?"* Many people will happily text even when they won't leave a voicemail, and now the conversation is alive instead of lost.

It costs a fraction of a receptionist, it works 24/7, and it gives the caller a reason to stay with you instead of dialing the next name. It's exactly what [Missed Call Text-Back](../features/missed-call-text-back.html) does inside The Peak System.

## 3. Cover after hours and weekends

Since a lot of homeowners call outside 9-to-5, whatever you use has to work at 8 p.m. on a Sunday. That rules out anything that only runs during business hours. An automatic text-back and a simple after-hours booking option mean the evening caller gets a response instead of silence, and a reason not to keep dialing.

## 4. Put a real answer on the line

For higher-value or complex calls, you may want an actual conversation, not just a text. Options range from a part-time receptionist to a call-answering service to a 24/7 automated answer that books the job and passes you the details. Each has trade-offs on cost and quality, but the principle is the same: *someone or something answers,* every time, so the caller never hits a dead end.

## 5. Track your missed calls so you know the size of the leak

Most contractors have no idea how many calls they miss, which means they can't see the money walking out the door. Simple call tracking shows you how many rang through, when, and from where. Once you can see the leak, fixing it stops being optional.

## The math that makes this a no-brainer

Do the math on your own business: take what an average job is worth to you and multiply it by the calls you miss in a month. Win back even two or three of those jobs and an automatic text-back has usually paid for itself. Everything after that is work you were already losing.

A great website brings the lead to your door (that's [half the job](contractor-website-not-getting-leads.html)). Answering the call is the other half. Do both, and the leads you work so hard to earn actually turn into work.
