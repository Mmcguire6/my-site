The phone rings while you're on a roof, under a sink, or driving to your next job. You can't answer. By the time you call back an hour later, they've already booked the contractor who picked up.

That one missed call just cost you around **$1,200** — the industry's average value of a single missed inbound call for a home-service business. And it's not a rare event. Studies of contractor phone lines find that roughly **62% of calls to home-service businesses go unanswered,** and as many as **85% of the people who reach voicemail never call back.** They don't leave a message. They dial the next name on Google.

Add it up and the average contractor bleeds **$800 to $1,500 every month** in work that simply rang through to voicemail. Here's why it happens — and five proven ways to stop it.

## Why the phone rings when you can't answer it

You're not missing calls because you're lazy. You're missing them because you can't be on a ladder and on the phone at the same time. The three biggest gaps:

- **You're on the job.** Hands full, loud site, gloves on. The one time of day a customer calls is the one time you can't talk.
- **It's after hours.** Around **73% of calls to home-service businesses come in outside standard business hours** — evenings and weekends, when a homeowner finally sits down and deals with that leak. A voicemail box is the only thing working that shift.
- **Everyone calls at once.** A storm rolls through, ten roofs need looking at, and the calls stack up faster than one person can answer.

None of that is a character flaw. It's just the math of running a trade. The problem is what happens to the call you miss.

## Why voicemail doesn't save you

Voicemail feels like a safety net. It isn't. Most people calling a contractor are calling three or four and hiring the first one who picks up or calls right back. Speed is the whole game: **the business that responds first wins the job the large majority of the time.**

So a missed call isn't a message waiting for you — it's a customer already talking to your competitor. Which means the fix isn't "check voicemail more often." It's making sure the call gets a response *now,* even when you can't give it. Here are five ways to do exactly that, roughly in order of payoff.

## 1. Call back within 5 minutes — every time

Speed-to-lead is the single biggest lever. Respond within five minutes and you're far more likely to reach the customer while they're still holding the phone and still deciding. Wait 30 minutes and most of that advantage is gone.

**The catch:** you can't reliably hit a five-minute callback when you're working. Which is exactly why the next four matter.

## 2. Text back every missed call — automatically

This is the highest-ROI fix on the list. The moment a call goes unanswered, an automatic text fires back to the caller: *"Sorry we missed you — this is [Your Company]. Can we help with a quote?"* Most people will happily text even when they won't leave a voicemail, and now the conversation is alive instead of lost.

It costs a fraction of a receptionist, it works 24/7, and it turns your biggest leak into a booked job. It's the core of our [missed-call text-back](../features/missed-call-text-back.html) system for exactly that reason.

## 3. Cover after hours and weekends

Since roughly three-quarters of calls land outside 9-to-5, whatever you use has to work at 8 p.m. on a Sunday. That rules out anything that only runs during business hours. An automatic text-back and a simple after-hours booking option mean the evening caller gets a response instead of silence — and a reason not to keep dialing.

## 4. Put a real answer on the line

For higher-value or complex calls, you may want an actual conversation, not just a text. Options range from a part-time receptionist to a call-answering service to a 24/7 automated answer that books the job and passes you the details. Each has trade-offs on cost and quality, but the principle is the same: *someone or something answers,* every time, so the caller never hits a dead end.

## 5. Track your missed calls so you know the size of the leak

Most contractors have no idea how many calls they miss — which means they can't see the money walking out the door. Simple call tracking shows you how many rang through, when, and from where. Once you can see the leak, fixing it stops being optional.

## The math that makes this a no-brainer

Say your average job is worth a few hundred dollars and you're missing even ten calls a month. Recovering just two or three of them covers the cost of any of the fixes above several times over — and everything after that is pure profit you were already losing.

A great website brings the lead to your door (that's [half the job](contractor-website-not-getting-leads.html)). Answering the call is the other half. Do both, and the leads you work so hard to earn actually turn into work.

Mostly, no. As many as **85% of callers who reach voicemail hang up without leaving a message,** and a large share immediately call a competitor instead. People treat a business voicemail as a dead end — which is why "just check your voicemail" isn't a fix. The call has to get a live response, or at least an instant text, before the caller moves on.

The exact number depends on your average job size, but even a handful of missed calls a month adds up to serious money over a year.
