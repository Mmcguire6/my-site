Ask what a small-business website costs and you'll get a straight answer: somewhere between **$0 and $35,000.** Useless, right? That range is technically true and completely unhelpful when you're just trying to figure out what's fair to pay for your shop, trade, or clinic here in Alberta.

So let's make it useful. Here's what you actually pay by route, what drives the number up or down, and — the part nobody warns you about — where the money quietly leaks after launch.

## What you'll pay, by route

There are really four ways to get a website, and they land at very different prices:

- **Do it yourself on a builder (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy):** roughly **$200 to $600 a year** including hosting. Cheapest on paper. You supply all the time, the design, and the copy — and these platforms are often slow and hard to rank.
- **Hire a freelancer:** about **$1,500 to $8,000** one-time for a custom small-business site. Quality swings wildly with the person.
- **Hire an agency:** **$6,000 to $35,000+** for strategy, custom design, and development. Great work at the top end; a lot of markup and bloat at the low end.
- **Managed monthly (design-build-host on a subscription):** from around **$97/month,** which spreads the cost and rolls hosting, updates, and support into one predictable bill instead of a big upfront hit.

Most professional builds land somewhere between **$3,000 and $15,000** if you're paying upfront — but the number swings hard based on what's actually in the site.

## What actually drives the price

Two "five-page websites" can cost $2,000 or $12,000 depending on:

- **Custom design vs. template.** A tuned, on-brand design costs more than dropping your logo into a stock theme — and usually converts better.
- **Number of pages.** A five-page brochure site is a fraction of a 30-page site with a page for every service and town you serve (the kind that actually [ranks and pulls leads](contractor-website-not-getting-leads.html)).
- **Functionality.** Online booking, payments, a customer login, or e-commerce each add real build time.
- **Copywriting and photos.** Words that sell and real photos of your work are often the difference between a site that converts and one that just sits there — and they cost either your time or someone's fee.
- **Speed and SEO done right.** A site built to [load fast](why-is-my-website-so-slow.html) and rank locally takes more skill than one thrown together in a page builder.

## The costs nobody warns you about

Here's the part that ambushes owners: the sticker price isn't the real price. Ongoing costs most people overlook add **$1,100 to $5,000 a year:**

- Hosting: anywhere from a few dollars to $120+ a month.
- Domain and SSL: small, but recurring.
- Plugins and subscriptions: booking tools, security, backups — the "just $15/month" add-ons that stack up.
- Maintenance: updates, fixes, and the inevitable "can you change this" that a freelancer bills hourly for.

A "cheap" site with $3,000 a year of hidden upkeep isn't cheap. Always ask what the **total** looks like over two or three years, not just the build fee.

## The trap at both ends

The two most expensive mistakes are at opposite ends of the price range:

- **Too cheap.** A $0 DIY site that's slow, generic, and never ranks doesn't save you money — it costs you every lead it fails to capture. The cheapest site is the most expensive one if it brings in nothing.
- **Overpaying for bloat.** On the other end, plenty of agencies charge $15,000 for a site built on the same heavy page-builder you could've rented for $30 a month — you're paying a premium for something that's slow by design.

The goal isn't the lowest price or the highest. It's the best **return:** a fast, clear, well-ranked site that brings in more work than it costs, without a five-figure bill or a pile of hidden fees.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a small-business website cost in 2026?

Most professional builds land between **$3,000 and $15,000** if you pay upfront, though the full range runs from about $200/year for a DIY builder to $35,000+ at a full-service agency. ### Why are website quotes so different for the same thing?

Because "a website" can mean wildly different builds. A templated five-page site and a custom, fast, 30-page site engineered to rank locally are different amounts of work, even if both are "a website." Quotes also vary by who does it — a solo freelancer, a boutique studio, or a big agency with overhead and markup. Always compare what's actually included: design, pages, copy, speed, SEO, and ongoing support.

### What are the ongoing costs of a website?

Beyond the build, plan for **$1,100 to $5,000 a year** in hosting, domain and SSL renewals, plugin and tool subscriptions, security, backups, and maintenance. These are the costs most owners forget, and they can quietly make a "cheap" site the expensive one. Ask any provider for the total cost over two to three years, not just the upfront fee.

### Is a cheap DIY website worth it?

It can work for a very simple presence, but for a business that needs leads it usually costs more than it saves. DIY builders tend to be slow, hard to rank, and generic — and a site that never brings in work is the most expensive option there is, no matter how little you paid for it. If the site is meant to generate customers, pay for speed, clarity, and local SEO.
