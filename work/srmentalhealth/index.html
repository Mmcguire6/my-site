№ 08 FAQ

Good to know

Psychiatry is a branch of medicine focused on the diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of mental, emotional, and behavioral disorders.

They are Board Certified professionals that specialize in mental health. They assess mental health conditions, provide treatment including therapeutic interventions and medication management, and offer support to individuals and families dealing with mental health issues.

Common conditions include depression, anxiety disorders (generalized anxiety, panic disorder, social anxiety), bipolar disorder, attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), among others. We are specialists in diagnosing and treating ADHD in women, as symptoms can manifest differently than in men.

Psychiatric nurse practitioners can prescribe medication and provide medical treatment for mental health disorders. Psychologists are trained in psychology and typically provide therapy and counseling services. Psychologists cannot prescribe medication, but they often work closely with psychiatric providers to deliver comprehensive care.

If you're experiencing persistent sadness, anxiety, changes in sleep or appetite, difficulty concentrating, or thoughts of self-harm, it may be beneficial to seek help. Also, if you struggle with executive function, focusing on tasks you don't enjoy, or procrastination, you may have ADHD — we can help.

We'll ask about your symptoms, medical history, family history, and current medications. We may ask about physical issues and order laboratory tests if necessary. Based on this information, we'll work with you to develop a treatment plan tailored to your needs.

Treatment depends on the severity of symptoms and their impact on daily functioning. Some individuals can manage mild symptoms with self-care techniques or therapy alone; others may require medication or more intensive treatment. Not everyone who goes on medication needs to stay on it long term.
