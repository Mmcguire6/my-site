Our Flagship

Collaborative Divorce

Collaborative Divorce

A legally binding commitment to stay out of court.

Collaborative Divorce is a way to transition families through the end of marriage while minimizing the many stresses this entails. Each party is represented by an attorney trained in non-adversarial conflict resolution, and the parties sign a legally binding agreement not to litigate — committing all energy to reaching the highest quality settlement through good-faith negotiation.

  • Legally binding commitment to stay out of court
  • Preserves co-parenting relationships and family goodwill
  • Achieves high-quality settlements while reducing conflict, expense and stress

Mediation

Mediation

A neutral guide to peaceful agreement.

In mediation, spouses work with Roy as a neutral third party trained in conflict resolution techniques to reach agreement on parenting, property division, spousal maintenance and other divorce-related issues. Unlike litigation, mediation keeps decision-making in the hands of the family — not the court.

  • Neutral, non-partisan process led by a trained mediator
  • Covers parenting, property division, spousal support and more
  • Typically faster, less expensive and less adversarial than court

07 / 11 — Approach Our Approach · Divorce With Love

Our Approach

Compassionate by design.

People going through the dissolution of their family are almost always basically good folks facing one of the most challenging experiences of their lives. The approaches on these pages exist precisely because remaining calm in the face of provocation is far easier said than done.

01

Principle One

Non-Adversarial by Choice

Avoiding the conflict, expense and stress of litigation.

I am a proponent of alternative dispute resolution techniques, including Mediation and Collaborative Divorce, with their emphasis on negotiated resolutions. The goal is to achieve the highest quality settlements while avoiding the conflict, expense and stress of litigation.

02

Principle Two

Protecting What Can't Be Measured

Intangibles are easily overlooked — and easily destroyed.

Families have both tangible and intangible interests. Tangible interests are those things that can be measured like pension plans and home equity. Intangible interests — the ability to co-parent, shared memories, a child's secure attachment — are all too easily overlooked.

03

Principle Three

A Voice of Reason

What you will think years from now, looking back with calmness.

I care far less about what my client believes in the moment than in what he or she is likely to think years from now, when looking back on the case with calmness and objectivity. The greater your rapport with your attorney, the easier it will be to hear and consider the advice offered.
