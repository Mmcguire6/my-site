10 / 12 — Questions

Frequently Asked

Answers, before you call.

Do I need an attorney?

If you’ve been arrested or charged with a crime — or the police want to talk to you about one — you likely need an attorney. You can represent yourself, but it’s rarely a good idea. Consultations for criminal cases in Toledo and the surrounding area are free.

How much is the initial consultation?

It’s free. It costs nothing to call or set up a time to meet, and we can talk through the facts of your case at that meeting.

I’ve been charged with a crime — now what?

The first step is getting legal representation. You’re allowed to represent yourself, but it’s rarely advisable. If you can’t afford counsel and the charge carries possible jail time, an attorney will be appointed for you.

Do I have attorney-client privilege?

Yes. When you discuss the facts of your case with an attorney with the intent of possibly hiring them, attorney-client privilege applies — even before you’ve formally retained anyone.

What is an arraignment?

An arraignment is the initial court appearance where you enter a plea. In Ohio, the typical pleas are guilty, not guilty, or no contest.

What is a no-contest plea?

A no-contest plea doesn’t mean you’re admitting guilt — it allows the judge to accept the facts stated in the complaint and affidavit as true.

Will I receive jail time?

It depends on the charge. Many Ohio offenses carry the possibility of jail, and some require a mandatory sentence on conviction. The outcome depends on the specific facts of your case.

I have a warrant — what do I do?

Generally, you need to turn yourself in to clear a bench warrant. Each court has its own procedures, and an attorney can help you navigate them and address the underlying matter.

How much will an attorney cost?

It depends on the severity of the charge and other factors. Keep in mind that a conviction can affect your employment, professional licensing, and educational opportunities — which makes representation a worthwhile investment.
