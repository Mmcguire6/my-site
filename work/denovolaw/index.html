Practice Areas

Every resolution path, under one roof.

From full court representation to collaborative divorce, legal coaching and unbundled services — Chantelle tailors the approach to your family's real situation and budget.

i.

Litigation

Litigation (i.e. court) is a dispute resolution option when the parties involved are not able to resolve the matter or reach a mutually agreeable resolution. Though Chantelle will generally advise you that court is the last place you want to go, she is skillful, diligent and extremely well-prepared when representing her clients in the court room.

CourtDivorceCustody
ii.

Mediation

Mediation is a confidential, private process in which an impartial person, a mediator, facilitates communication between both parties and attempts to promote mutually agreeable settlement of the issues. Mediation is an informal, voluntary and confidential process.

Out-of-CourtSettlementPrivate
iii.

Collaborative Divorce

In this process, four-way meetings are used to resolve issues and stay out of court. Chantelle is trained in collaborative divorce and offers this process as a choice for her clients, in order to create as respectful an atmosphere as possible and create more options for reaching win-win solutions for both spouses.

CooperativeNo CourtRespectful
iv.

Legal Coaching

This process allows you to represent yourself with the coaching and help of a lawyer. Chantelle is trained in legal coaching and believes this approach represents an affordable means of accessing legal information and advice for those who cannot afford the traditional full-representation retainer model.

AffordableSelf-RepGuidance
v.

Unbundled Services

In this process, you represent yourself for some steps and hire a lawyer for other steps. A flexible and cost-effective way to access legal representation only where you need it most.

When parents separate, their children still require support. In Canada, there are guidelines to govern this issue. The amount of child support payable will depend on the parenting schedule in place.

Specialty Practice

When children need their own voice.

Children can have lawyers too. While most family law disputes can be resolved without a child having their own lawyer, it is possible — and sometimes essential — for children to have their own legal representation. The child's lawyer will advocate for the child's wishes and position.

It is fairly common for children in protection matters to have counsel appointed to act on their behalf. Children caught in a custody and access dispute between parents can also have a lawyer — a way of providing an independent voice for those who need it most.
